DEMO KNOWLEDGE DOCUMENT — Soil and Water Management
Source status: Illustrative starter content for testing retrieval only. Replace with reviewed ICAR / State Agricultural University / Kisan Call Centre material before operational use.

Soil testing helps farmers understand soil properties and nutrient status before making fertilizer decisions. A representative soil sample should be collected using the sampling method recommended by the local soil testing laboratory or agriculture department. Avoid mixing visibly unusual patches with the rest of the field unless they are sampled separately.

Keep the soil test report with the field record. Fertilizer and soil amendment decisions should be based on the crop, soil test results, local recommendations and the expected yield target. Do not apply a generic fertilizer dose from an unrelated region or crop.

Water management depends on crop type, growth stage, soil texture, rainfall, field drainage and local irrigation conditions. Monitor soil moisture and crop condition, and avoid both prolonged waterlogging and unnecessary irrigation. Local extension guidance should be used to determine crop-specific irrigation scheduling.

Record irrigation dates, rainfall, field observations and input applications. For location-specific fertilizer or irrigation advice, consult the relevant State Agricultural University, ICAR source or local agriculture extension officer.
